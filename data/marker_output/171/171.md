THE WAY FORWARD
Peter J. M. Van Haastert* and Peter N. Devreotes*
During random locomotion, human neutrophils and Dictyostelium discoideum amoebae
repeatedly extend and retract cytoplasmic processes. During directed cell migration —
chemotaxis — these pseudopodia form predominantly at the leading edge in response to the
local accumulation of certain signalling molecules. Concurrent changes in actin and myosin
enable the cell to move towards the stimulus. Recent studies are beginning to identify an intricate
network of signalling molecules that mediate these processes, and how these molecules become
localized in the cell is now becoming clear.
