Several models for gradient sensing by eukaryotic cells
have been proposed (Devreotes and Zigmond, 1988;
Caterina and Devreotes, 1991). First, a “purely temporal”
model depicts cells as extending pilot sensors in all
directions, responding only to those extended up the
gradient. Second, a “spatial-temporal” model sees cells
as requiring temporal increases in receptor occupancy
to respond, but also needs inhibition to occur at the
side of the cell receiving a later or lower stimulus. Third,
a “purely spatial” model has cells capable of intrinsically
detecting differences in receptor occupancy between
its two ends, even when receptor occupancy shows
no temporal change. Our findings rule out the “purely
temporal” model since nonmotile cells are able to po-
larize their signal transduction responses (Figure 6).
Rather, our results favor the “purely spatial” model. For
spatial detection, cells must sense small differences in
receptor occupancy at their two ends. Hence, the need
