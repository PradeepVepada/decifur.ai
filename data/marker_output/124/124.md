Controlling Aggregation and Postaggregative
Development in Dictyostelium

Geoffrey Pitt,† Peter N. Devreotes,† and Richard A. Firtel*,1
*Department of Biology, Center for Molecular Genetics, University of California, San Diego,

We have examined the role of cAMP-dependent protein kinase (PKA) in controlling aggregation and postaggregative develop-
ment in Dictyostelium. We previously showed that cells in which the gene encoding the PKA catalytic subunit has been
disrupted (pkacat0 cells) are unable to aggregate [S. K. O. Mann and R. A. Firtel (1991). A developmentally regulated,
putative serine/threonine protein kinase is essential for development in Dictyostelium. Mech. Dev. 35, 89–102]. We show
that pkacat0 cells are unable to activate adenylyl cyclase in response to cAMP stimulation due to the inability to express
the aggregation-stage, G-protein-stimulated adenylyl cyclase (ACA). Constitutive expression of ACA from an actin promoter
results in a high level of Mn2/-stimulated adenylyl cyclase activity and restores chemoattractant- and GTPgS-stimulated
adenylyl cyclase activity but not the ability to aggregate. Similarly, expression of the constitutively active, non-G protein-
coupled adenylyl cyclase ACG in pkacat0 cells also does not restore the ability to aggregate, although ACG can complement
cells in which the ACA gene has been disrupted. These results indicate that pkacat0 cells lack multiple, essential aggrega-
tion-stage functions. As the mound forms, high, continuous levels of extracellular cAMP functioning through the cAMP
serpentine receptors activate a transcriptional cascade that leads to cell-type differentiation and morphogenesis. The ﬁrst
step is the induction and activation of the transcription factor GBF and downstream postaggregative genes, followed by
the induction of prestalk- and prespore-speciﬁc genes. We show that pkacat0 cells induce postaggregative gene expression
in response to exogenous cAMP, but the level of induction of some of these genes, including GBF, is reduced. SP60 (a
prespore-speciﬁc gene) is not induced and ecmA (a prestalk-speciﬁc gene) is induced to very low levels. Expressing GBF
constitutively in pkacat0 cells restores ecmA expression to a moderate level, but SP60 is not detectably induced. Overex-
pression of PKAcat from the Actin 15 (Act15), ecmA prestalk, and the PKAcat promoters in pkacat0 cells results in
signiﬁcant aberrant spatial patterning of prestalk and prespore cells, as determined by lacZ reporter studies. Our studies
identify new, essential regulatory roles for PKA in mediating multicellular development.
