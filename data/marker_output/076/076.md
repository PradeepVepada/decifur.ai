In conclusion, 
the discovery of three additional Ga genes in Dicfyosfelium 
suggests 
that this primitive 
multicellular 
microorganism 
expresses 
at least eight 
G-protein 
a- 
subunits. 
More novel a-subunits 
might 
exist whose sequence 
is divergent 
in the 
conserved 
regions such that our PCR techniques 
would not have detected 
them. 
In 
mammalian 
system, there are 17 Ga subunits identified 
(20). The remarkable 
degree of 
similarity 
in the mechanisms 
of signal transduction 
among 
Dictyostelium 
and higher 
eukaryotes 
makes this biochemically 
and genetically 
accessible 
organism 
an attractive 
model system to study G-protein function.
